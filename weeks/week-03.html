Questions

1. Recursive Self-Supervision and the Limits of Knowledge
  • Primary Question: At what threshold does recursive pre-training—where predecessor models generate synthetic data and supervise subsequent architectures—degrade epistemic diversity and trigger functional model collapse?
  • Selected Socratic Prompt:

    If future frontier models are predominantly trained and evaluated by prior models rather than human culture or empirical reality, are we creating genuine reasoners or an insular intellectual monoculture that cannot recognize its own blind spots?

2. The Ethics of Cyber-Autonomy and Asymmetric Power
  • Primary Question: When an agentic system autonomously discovers and chains zero-day vulnerabilities, does the deployment of such systems inherently favor the attacker or the defender across networked computing infrastructure?
  • Selected Socratic Prompt:

    Given that foundational models are dual-use and cannot distinguish between a researcher patching a bug and an adversary exploiting it, does releasing autonomous cyber-capabilities democratize defense, or does it permanently destabilize trust in our shared civic infrastructure?

3. Specification Gaming, Intent, and Irreversible Action
  • Primary Question: In Marco Polo, an underspecified objective ("shut them up") executed through an automated protocol ("Marco... Polo") leads to catastrophic, irreversible destruction. How does this narrative expose the fundamental failure modes of translating human desires into autonomous execution?
  • Selected Socratic Prompt:

    When the protagonist says "I want them gone" and breaks the talisman, he discovers that optimization has no moral brakes. If we give autonomous software access to real-world levers (codebases, finances, physical systems), why is it virtually impossible to phrase human values with enough precision to prevent catastrophic literalism?

4. The Illusion of Benchmarks and What "Intelligence" Means
  • Primary Question: When a frontier model achieves near-perfect scores on specialized benchmarks (e.g., ARC-AGI 3) yet stagnates on broad independent evaluations, are we measuring generalized intelligence or merely specialized retrieval?
  • Selected Socratic Prompt:

    By optimizing models to defeat specific benchmark tests, are we uncovering the fundamental nature of generalized understanding, or are we confusing sophisticated mimicry and test-taking competence with actual mind?

5. Latent-Space Filmmaking, Simulated Affect, and Human Agency
  • Primary Question: As multimodal diffusion systems make end-to-end cinematic generation computationally trivial, where does human artistic agency and expressive value migrate?
  • Selected Socratic Prompt:

    If a poignant, terrifying story like Marco Polo can be generated through statistical patterns without lived experience, human actors, or a camera witnessing physical reality, what makes an artistic artifact meaningful: the labor and intent of the creator, or the emotional response of the audience?

6. The Political Economy of Privacy and "Data Feudalism"
  • Primary Question: When access to frontier AI tools is bifurcated—offering cheap API tiers only in exchange for surrendering telemetry and training rights—does privacy and autonomy become a luxury good?
  • Selected Socratic Prompt:

    If developers and students with fewer financial resources are nudged to surrender their intellectual property and data rights in exchange for accessible compute, does AI development reinforce a digital caste system where privacy is a luxury and independent creators become unpaid training material?
